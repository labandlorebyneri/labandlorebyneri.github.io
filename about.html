About Neri

Professionally curious about why people do what they do, how ideas take shape, and what makes communities move.

I am Joshua Neri Aguilar Cristobal, the person behind Lab & Lore by Neri.

Where the curiosity began

Much of what I know about people began long before the classroom.

I was born and raised in a household of love, the proud child of a farmer and a public market vendor. Growing up, service was simply part of life. I served the church and found different ways to contribute through singing, dancing, public speaking, organizing events, and helping with charitable work.

Those experiences taught me that people are rarely explained by a single reason. What someone believes, creates, or contributes is shaped by where they come from, what they have lived through, who surrounds them, and what they believe is possible.

I was also shaped by three women from their generation who, in different ways, showed me what care, strength, and responsibility could look like.

Jenet, my selfless mother, taught me the quiet discipline of showing up for the people you love.

Imelda, my loving aunt, showed me another expression of family, generosity, and presence.

And Geraldine Roman, a mother to me from another bloodline, expanded my understanding of belonging, possibility, and the many ways one person can leave an imprint on another.

Their influence is part of why I remain interested not only in what people achieve, but in what shapes them.

A language for the questions

Psychology gave that curiosity a more formal language.

I studied Bachelor of Arts in Psychology at the University of the Philippines Diliman, where that curiosity found a more formal language through behavior, motivation, identity, social context, and the many forces that shape how people think and act.

My work since then has moved across research, strategy, communication, projects, programs, public speaking, organizational questions, technology, and community work. Different settings keep bringing me back to similar questions: Why do people behave the way they do? What shapes their choices? Why do some ideas travel while others disappear? What helps people move together?

I have always been drawn to the space between analysis and intuition, between what evidence tells us and what lived experience complicates.

Why Lab & Lore

Some questions become more useful when they meet real life.

Lab & Lore grew out of that curiosity.

I am interested in systems, but also in the people who have to live inside them. I care about strategy, but also about culture. I value data, but I am equally interested in the stories, relationships, assumptions, and contradictions that numbers cannot fully explain on their own.

Across psychology, work, service, creative pursuits, and everyday life, I kept returning to three ideas.

The Three Cs

Character. Creativity. Community.

The core lenses through which I explore people, ideas, and collective life.

01

Character

Who we become. Character is about identity, values, behavior, choice, motivation, growth, and the experiences that shape a person. It asks what sits underneath what people say and do.

02

Creativity

How we respond. Creativity is not limited to art. It is the ability to imagine alternatives, question assumptions, communicate differently, experiment, solve problems, and build something that did not exist before.

03

Community

How we move together. Community is where individual choices encounter other people, institutions, traditions, incentives, expectations, and shared responsibilities. It is where ideas are tested by participation, trust, conflict, cooperation, and collective action.

The point of the inquiry

Look more closely.

Together, the Three Cs became the framework through which I explore the ideas published in Lab & Lore.

The goal is not to arrive at a final answer to every question.

It is to look more closely.

To understand why people behave the way they do. To examine what happens when ideas meet reality. To notice the forces that help people move together, and the ones that keep them apart.

Lab & Lore is where I make room for that curiosity.

How I explore questions

Observe. Question. Test. Reflect.

Lab & Lore is not a catalogue of conclusions. It is a record of inquiry.

Observe

Notice behavior, language, incentives, contradictions, environments, systems, patterns, and details that are easy to dismiss.

Question

Challenge explanations that feel too convenient and assumptions that survive mostly because nobody has disturbed them lately.

Test

Compare alternatives through research, experimentation, implementation, conversation, evidence, and the useful inconvenience of being wrong.

Reflect

Ask what the result changes, what deserves to be remembered, and what new question appeared along the way.

Continue exploring

The questions continue in the writing.

Observations, experiments, arguments, and reflections across Character, Creativity, and Community.
