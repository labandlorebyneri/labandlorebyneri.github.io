About Neri C

Professionally curious about why people do what they do.

I am Neri C, a creative behavioral scientist interested in the point where human behavior, creativity, research, technology, and real-world problems meet.

My path has crossed research, digital marketing, strategy, project and program management, communication, public speaking, organizational questions, and community work.

Different settings. Similar questions. How do people think, decide, create, organize, and interact with the systems and people around them?

The Path Behind the Questions

I have never been particularly interested in staying inside one lane.

I studied at the University of the Philippines Diliman, where research became more than an academic exercise for me. It became a way of approaching uncertainty.

Since then, my work has moved through different kinds of problems, organizations, audiences, and systems. Research, marketing, technology, strategy, project development, communication, and community involvement may look like different disciplines.

I tend to notice what connects them.

People make decisions inside systems. Groups develop norms. Organizations create incentives. Technology changes what is possible. Communication changes what people believe is possible.

That is the territory I like exploring.

Research

Questions before conclusions

Research, to me, means being curious enough to ask, structured enough to investigate, and flexible enough to change an answer when the evidence changes.

Strategy

Understanding before action

Marketing, communication, and strategy interest me because good ideas alone are rarely enough. You have to understand people well enough to know which ideas deserve to become actions.

Projects & Programs

Where ideas meet reality

Projects expose the difference between an interesting idea and a workable one. Resources, incentives, institutions, timing, behavior, and coordination all enter the equation.

People & Community

Behavior is rarely a solo act

Public speaking, professional conversations, and community involvement continually remind me that behavior becomes clearer when people are participants in the conversation, not simply objects of observation.

My Framework

Character. Creativity. Community.

Over time, many of the questions I encounter have started to organize themselves around three connected lenses. I call them the Three Cs.

01 · The Individual

Character

Who we become.

Character explores the human psyche, identity, interests, attitudes, values, morality, pain, suffering, decision-making, personal growth, leadership, and the experiences that shape an individual.

02 · The Response

Creativity

How we respond.

Creativity explores marketing, communication, experimentation, research, problem solving, organizational behavior, and the alternative responses we create when familiar answers stop being useful.

03 · The Collective

Community

How we move together.

Community examines group thinking, group dynamics, organizations, hierarchy, leadership, social and economic mobility, institutions, collective behavior, and movements.

How I Explore Questions

Observe. Question. Test. Reflect.

Lab & Lore is not a catalogue of conclusions. It is a record of inquiry.

Observe

Notice behavior, language, incentives, contradictions, environments, systems, patterns, and the details that are easy to dismiss.

Question

Challenge explanations that feel too convenient, distinctions that seem too clean, and assumptions that survive mostly because nobody has disturbed them lately.

Test

Compare alternatives through research, experimentation, implementation, conversation, evidence, and occasionally the useful inconvenience of being wrong.

Reflect

Ask what the result changes about the original question, what deserves to be remembered, and what new question appeared along the way.

Why Lab & Lore

The lab investigates. The lore remembers.

The lab represents inquiry. It is where I observe, question, experiment, research, challenge assumptions, change my mind, and occasionally discover that I was asking the wrong question in the first place.

The lore represents what survives that process. It is the lesson, contradiction, interpretation, story, experience, or realization worth carrying forward.

Character, Creativity, and Community give me three ways to understand the world. The lab gives me a way to examine them. The lore gives me somewhere to preserve what I learn.

What is understood in the lab becomes lore for another generation to encounter, question, challenge, and build upon.
