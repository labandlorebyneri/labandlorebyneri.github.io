Course context

English 13 · University of the Philippines

This paper was written for English 13 at the University of the Philippines under the theme Gender and Identity.

The assignment used argumentative writing as a way of developing a position, organizing evidence, and testing how an idea could be sustained across a longer academic essay.

Central argument

Gender influences the leadership compass of politicians.

The essay argues that gender influences political leadership through three broad areas: demeanor, performance, and the handling of relationships.

It develops that argument by comparing how male and female political leaders were described in relation to decision-making, emotional management, language, policy preferences, programs, crisis response, and local or international relations.

How the argument develops

The paper compares political leadership through three lenses.

These are claims made by the English 13 essay and are presented here as part of the paper's original argument.

01

Demeanor

The paper argues that gender influences decision-making, emotional management, diction, and delivery. It associates men with more systematic and direct styles and women with more empathetic and audience-conscious styles.

02

Performance

The essay argues that gender can influence the laws, programs, and responses leaders prioritize. It associates women more strongly with welfare, education, equality, and environmental concerns, and men with security and governance.

03

Relationships

The paper argues that gender also affects local and international relations, particularly how leaders communicate approval, disagreement, negotiation, and concern for an audience.

Conclusion

The essay concludes that gender is one influence on political leadership, alongside other social and cultural factors.

The paper concludes that gender influences how politicians think, act, communicate, make policy, and manage relationships.

It also states that biological make-up and social, cultural, and political values affect a politician's leadership compass.

The essay ends by returning responsibility to citizens, arguing that voters should choose leaders who can feel with the people, serve selflessly, and work toward a better country.

Sources & approach

The essay combines secondary sources with comparative examples.

The paper draws from books, journal articles, reports, news coverage, and institutional sources, then applies those references to examples involving political leaders and public issues.

Its method is argumentative rather than empirical: sources are used to support and organize a thesis rather than to produce original research data.
