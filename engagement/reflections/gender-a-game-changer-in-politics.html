Gender: A Game Changer in Politics

An English 13: Writing as Thinking essay written under the theme Gender and Identity, examining how gender was argued to shape political leadership through demeanor, performance, and relationships.

← Back to Essays

Course context

English 13 · Writing as Thinking

This paper was written as a course requirement for English 13 under the theme Gender and Identity.

The assignment used argumentative writing as a way of developing a position, organizing evidence, and testing how an idea could be sustained across a longer academic essay.

Central argument

Gender influences the leadership compass of politicians.

The essay argues that gender influences political leadership through three broad areas: demeanor, performance, and the handling of relationships.

It develops that argument by comparing how male and female political leaders were described in relation to decision-making, emotional management, language, policy preferences, programs, crisis response, and local or international relations.

How the argument develops

Three lenses organize the essay.

The page preserves the structure and claims of the original academic essay rather than revising them from a later point of view.

01

Demeanor

The essay compares decision-making, emotional management, diction, and delivery as ways gender might shape political behavior.

02

Performance

It examines laws, programs, projects, and responses to national problems as visible expressions of leadership.

03

Relationships

It considers how leaders manage local and international relations and how communication style affects those interactions.

Sources & approach

The essay combines secondary sources with comparative examples.

The paper draws from books, journal articles, reports, news coverage, and institutional sources, then applies those references to examples involving political leaders and public issues.

Its method is argumentative rather than empirical: sources are used to support and organize a thesis rather than to produce original research data.
