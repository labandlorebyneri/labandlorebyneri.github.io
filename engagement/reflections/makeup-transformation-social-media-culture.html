Makeup Transformation and Social Media Culture

An excerpt from a collaborative Grade 12 Media and Information Literacy paper examining how social media, celebrity imitation, cosmetics, and before-and-after transformation culture shape beauty, confidence, and online self-presentation.

Academic context
Grade 12 · Media and Information Literacy · St. John Academy
Essay type
Excerpt from a collaborative group paper
Theme
Makeup transformation and social media culture
Central argument
Celebrity and social media culture helped turn makeup transformation into a shared form of online expression while also shaping beauty and confidence.
Structure
Celebrity influence → makeup and confidence → before-and-after transformation → online humor

The phenomenon

Makeup transformation is treated as a form of social-media culture.

The excerpt begins from the idea that social media can establish standards that people imitate and circulate. Within that environment, makeup transformation is presented as the use of cosmetics to resemble celebrities, public figures, or an idealized version of oneself.

The paper argues that mass sharing and public attention helped move makeup transformation beyond a private beauty practice and into the realm of recognizable popular culture.

Celebrity influence

Imitation helped make transformation visible and repeatable.

The excerpt uses celebrity-inspired transformations to show how recognizable faces can become templates for online participation. Examples in the original document include transformations modeled after Khloe Kardashian and Dakota Johnson.

These examples are used to argue that celebrity visibility encouraged other people to explore cosmetics as an artistic and performative medium.

Makeup and confidence

Cosmetics are presented as both enhancement and dependence.

The paper notes that makeup had become increasingly integrated into everyday routines rather than being reserved only for special occasions. It identifies appearance, presentability, and confidence as common reasons for using cosmetics.

At the same time, the excerpt raises a concern about dependence, asking what happens when confidence becomes tied too closely to wearing makeup rather than to comfort with one's unaltered appearance.

Transformation formats

The phenomenon extends beyond celebrity mimicry.

The second part of the excerpt discusses before-and-after transformation photographs and videos. In these formats, participants contrast an initial appearance with a transformed or idealized one.

The paper also observes that transformation formats became material for humor and sarcasm online, showing how beauty practices could simultaneously function as self-presentation, performance, and entertainment.
