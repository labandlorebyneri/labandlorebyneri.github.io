Lost and Found and Lost Again!

An English 13: Writing as Thinking essay written under the theme Nationhood, examining technology, education, media, youth, culture, and the development of Filipino national identity.

← Back to Essays

Course context

English 13 · Writing as Thinking

This paper was written as a course requirement for English 13 under the theme Nationhood.

The paper uses argumentative writing to ask how technology affects the development of Filipino nationhood, especially among young people.

Central argument

Technology can weaken or strengthen nationhood depending on how it is used.

The essay argues that technology affects Filipino nationhood through education, media, cultural consumption, and the role of young people as digital natives.

It presents technology as both a pressure on national identity and a possible instrument for strengthening knowledge of Filipino culture, history, language, and shared identity.

How the argument develops

The essay moves across institutions, platforms, and generations.

Each section asks how cultural identity is shaped by what people learn, consume, repeat, and share.

01

Education

The paper treats schools and language as major sites where ideas about country, culture, and identity are formed.

02

Technology

Digital tools are described as making learning and cultural exposure easier while also increasing exposure to non-Filipino influences.

03

Media

Social, broadcast, and print media are examined as channels capable of shaping shared ideas about Filipino identity and culture.

04

Youth

The essay gives young Filipinos a central role in using digital platforms to transmit culture, history, and national identity.

Sources & approach

The paper combines cultural critique with secondary sources.

The essay draws from books, reports, news coverage, web sources, and media studies references to build an argument about education, technology, culture, and nationhood.

Its approach is interpretive and argumentative rather than empirical. The sources support a broader cultural argument about how national identity is learned and reproduced.
