Course context

English 13 · University of the Philippines

This paper was written for English 13 at the University of the Philippines under the theme Nationhood.

The paper uses argumentative writing to ask how technology affects the development of Filipino nationhood, especially among young people.

Central argument

Technology can weaken or strengthen nationhood depending on how it is used.

The essay argues that technology affects Filipino nationhood through education, media, cultural consumption, and the role of young people as digital natives.

It presents technology as both a pressure on national identity and a possible instrument for strengthening knowledge of Filipino culture, history, language, and shared identity.

How the argument develops

The paper connects nationhood to education, technology, media, and youth.

The sections below summarize the positions advanced by the English 13 essay in its original context.

01

Education

The essay argues that Philippine education inherited strong American influence and that prioritizing English and global competitiveness can weaken attachment to Filipino language, culture, and national identity.

02

Technology

Technology is presented as both a source of foreign cultural influence and a tool that can make learning, cultural transmission, and the promotion of Filipino identity more accessible.

03

Media and popular culture

The paper argues that social, broadcast, and print media help set cultural standards. It treats strong American and Korean influence as pressures on Filipino identity and calls for greater representation of Filipino traditions, history, and culture.

04

Youth

Filipino youth and social-media creators are positioned as important carriers of nationhood because their familiarity with digital media can be used to share Filipino history, language, traditions, and values.

Conclusion

The essay argues that technology should be used to strengthen rather than displace Filipino identity.

The paper concludes that technology has a substantial role in the development of Filipino nationhood because of its integration with education and its influence through media.

It argues that technological progress should be used as an opportunity to build nationalism and nationhood rather than simply reproduce foreign cultural standards.

Its final position is that Filipino identity must ultimately be recovered and sustained by Filipinos themselves: the identity may have been found before, but the paper warns that it can be lost again.

Sources & approach

The paper combines cultural critique with secondary sources.

The essay draws from books, reports, news coverage, web sources, and media studies references to build an argument about education, technology, culture, and nationhood.

Its approach is interpretive and argumentative rather than empirical. The sources support a broader cultural argument about how national identity is learned and reproduced.
