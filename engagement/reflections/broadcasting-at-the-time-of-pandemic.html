Broadcasting at the Time of Pandemic

A pandemic-era essay examining Filipino BL series through political economy, audience power, representation, genre, programming, and the visibility of LGBTQIA experiences in emerging media.

Context

The paper begins with the problem of producing broadcast content during the pandemic.

The essay describes the pandemic as a disruption to broadcast-media production, forcing content producers to recycle material or find new ways to continue providing entertainment.

Within that setting, it identifies the rise of Filipino Boys' Love series on emerging platforms such as YouTube as a significant shift in the broadcasting landscape. Gameboys and Gaya sa Pelikula serve as the paper's primary examples.

Central argument

BL series became a site where media power, audience agency, representation, and equality intersected.

The paper argues that the significance of BL series cannot be understood only through their stories. Their casting, production, commercial decisions, audience reception, platform distribution, and social relevance also reveal how media power operates.

At the same time, the essay treats audiences as active participants who can reward, support, and expand the reach of particular programs through their viewing choices.

How the argument develops

Four ideas organize the paper's analysis.

These sections summarize the positions advanced by the essay in its original pandemic-era context.

01

Political economy & representation

The essay argues that economic power affects who gets cast, who gets to write, and which forms of representation are considered commercially viable. It uses the casting of cisgender male actors in LGBT roles and the role of writers and producers as examples of this power.

02

Audience power

The paper argues that viewers also exercise power by deciding which series to watch, support, and help circulate. It presents this as a tension between the influence of media producers and the agency of audiences.

03

Social relevance & equality

The essay situates the popularity of BL series alongside contemporaneous advocacy around the SOGIE Equality Bill and argues that socially relevant content gave calls for equality additional visibility in emerging media.

04

Genre & programming

The paper classifies BL series as drama and also as educational content. It discusses scheduling, producers, promotion, YouTube distribution, audience access, sponsorship, and expansion to other digital platforms as parts of programming strategy.

Works discussed

Gameboys and Gaya sa Pelikula anchor the analysis.

Gameboys is used to discuss the emergence of Filipino BL programming through online distribution and the audience attention generated around its actors and story.

Gaya sa Pelikula is discussed through its treatment of self-discovery, family, identity, and social attitudes toward LGBTQIA people. The essay treats scenes involving rejection and hurtful language as examples of how entertainment can also communicate social lessons.

Conclusion

Broadcast media was presented as both entertainment and a force that can shape social life.

The essay concludes that broadcast media played a vital role during a difficult year by continuing to produce content and by giving visibility to groups described in the paper as vulnerable or underrepresented.

Its final position is hopeful: as media continues to move forward, the writer expresses the same hope for equality and acceptance.

Sources & approach

The essay combines media concepts with contemporary examples.

The analysis applies ideas about political economy, audience power, genre, programming, and representation to pandemic-era Filipino BL series.

The submitted paper cites reporting on Gameboys and a source discussing the rise and history of Boys' Love media in Thailand, while using Gameboys and Gaya sa Pelikula as its central examples.
