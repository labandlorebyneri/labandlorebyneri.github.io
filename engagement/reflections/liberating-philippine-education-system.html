An Essay on a Liberating Philippine Education System

A PI 100 essay arguing that a truly liberating Philippine education system requires more than changes in teaching method or curriculum: it must also confront the social conditions that constrain learning.

Academic context
PI 100 · University of the Philippines
Essay type
Midterm examination essay
Year
2021
Central ideas
Education, language, nationalism, inequality, curriculum, facilities, and social reform
Structure
Educational assessments → language → socioeconomic conditions → facilities and curriculum → proposed reforms

Central argument

A liberating education system requires social reform beyond the classroom.

The essay argues that changing teaching methods alone cannot make Philippine education effective and liberating. It places educational outcomes within wider social conditions such as poverty, discrimination, hunger, inadequate medical services, and employment.

Its position is that educational competence becomes more attainable only when these structural conditions are also addressed.

Pakiwari ng Iilan

The paper begins with public reactions to international education assessments.

The essay discusses Philippine performance in international assessments such as PISA and TIMSS and presents several reactions from education officials, legislators, academics, and other commentators.

It also questions whether global assessments can be understood without examining their standards, language, and fit with the Philippine educational context.

Mga Suliranin at Pagsusuri

The essay identifies several layers of the education problem.

The paper treats poor educational performance as the result of overlapping linguistic, socioeconomic, institutional, and curricular conditions rather than a single failure.

01

Wika

The essay argues that English can become a barrier to learning because students must first process an acquired language before fully engaging with the lesson. It supports stronger use of primary and local languages while recognizing that language alone cannot resolve the education crisis.

02

Sosyo-ekonomikong kalagayan

The paper connects educational performance with poverty, hunger, employment, healthcare, attendance, gender, and access to learning resources. Education is presented as inseparable from the material conditions in which students live.

03

Pasilidad

The essay points to shortages in classrooms, facilities, and learning materials as practical barriers to implementing educational reforms and sustaining student participation.

04

Kurikulum

The paper questions the effectiveness of the K-12 curriculum and the spiral approach when students remain concentrated on fundamental concepts and teachers and schools lack sufficient support.

Sa Pagsulong

The essay calls for a multi-sectoral and multi-year approach to reform.

The proposed reforms include sustained teacher training, stronger use of primary languages in instruction, additional classrooms and learning facilities, and better-quality educational materials.

The essay also argues that educational reform requires addressing poverty itself, including hunger, discrimination, lack of healthcare, insecure work, and inadequate wages.

Conclusion

Liberating education is tied to a broader vision of social development.

The essay concludes that scholarships and subsidies alone are insufficient when students continue to face wider social deprivation. Educational reform must therefore move together with reforms in employment, health, food security, facilities, and other basic conditions.

Its final position is that Philippine education can become genuinely liberating and globally competitive only when the social problems that obstruct learning are also resolved.
