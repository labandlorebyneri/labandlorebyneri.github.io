As a National Discipline Awardee, What Can I Do to Make This Country a Better Place to Live In?

An essay I was selected to submit for the AY Foundation award, centered on discipline, responsibility, faith, education, and service.

← Back to Leadership & Development

Context

The prompt asked me to explain what discipline could contribute to the country.

I wrote this essay after being selected to submit an entry for the AY Foundation award. The prompt asked what I could do, as a National Discipline Awardee, to help make the country a better place to live in.

My answer was rooted in the world I knew at the time: school, church, family, academic work, student organizations, and youth service.

The essay is brief, but it captures how I understood discipline at that point in my life: as consistency between values, responsibilities, and action.

Discipline and character

Discipline meant consistency between values, responsibilities, and action.

I described a disciplined person as someone who worked hard, embodied personal ideals and morals, fulfilled responsibilities as a citizen, and lived with integrity.

At the time, I connected discipline strongly with meeting expectations: performing well in school, honoring commitments to church and community, balancing academics with co-curricular work, and setting a good example for other young people.

Discipline, in that framing, was not only self-control. It was a visible pattern of reliability.

The values underneath it

The essay tied achievement to something larger than achievement itself.

Even in a short award essay, four ideas were already connected: education, faith, family, and service.

01

Education

Academic excellence was framed as preparation: a way of developing ability so it could later be used in service of something larger.

02

Faith

Church commitments and Christian values were part of how I understood responsibility, character, and the obligation to serve.

03

Family

Doing well in school was also described as gratitude to parents whose work made education possible.

04

Service

Leadership was expressed through commitments to school, church, community, and the young people I was already serving.

Discipline as responsibility

The future depended on what I was willing to practice in the present.

One of the clearest ideas in the original essay was that future contribution begins with present behavior.

I did not describe nation-building through a grand plan. I described it through smaller commitments: studying well, keeping promises, serving consistently, honoring one's values, and becoming someone other people could rely on.

That idea remains useful to me. Character is often visible long before authority arrives.
