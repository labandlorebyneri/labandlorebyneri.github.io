Context

Psych 108 · University of the Philippines

This essay was written for Psych 108 at the University of the Philippines, drawing from a Lingkod Aral experience with He Cares Mission, an organization caring for children and supporting children in the surrounding community.

I joined as a volunteer teacher. Sessions included energizers, lessons, activities, meals, conversation, and the slower work of becoming familiar enough with one another for the children to participate more freely.

The placement was cut short when the pandemic and community quarantine began, but the experience had already become a way of testing ideas I had encountered in psychology against an actual relationship.

Changes identified in the reflection

The paper describes psychological, social, mental, emotional, and spiritual change.

Psychologically and socially, the reflection emphasizes flexibility, pakikibagay, pakikiramdam, and learning how to approach children with different experiences and circumstances.

Mentally, the paper describes the placement as strengthening resolve and clarifying a sense of purpose. Emotionally, it gives particular attention to empathy and malasakit, connecting the children's stories and daily struggles with a stronger desire to help.

Spiritually, the reflection describes the experience as renewing a faith life that had weakened after moving away from home.

Theory in lived experience

The reflection applies Filipino Psychology concepts to relationships formed during service.

The original reflection used Filipino Psychology to interpret what I was observing, particularly kapwa and the changing level of relationship between volunteer and child.

01

Kapwa

The reflection connected everyday concern for family and others to the idea of kapwa as a core Filipino value shaping social relations.

02

Pakikiramdam

Working with children required reading tone, hesitation, comfort, participation, and the social atmosphere instead of relying only on the lesson plan.

03

Pakikisama

The essay noticed how quieter forms of accommodation and adjustment helped sustain interaction while trust was still being built.

04

Relationship deepening

Using the Iskala ng Pagtutunguhan ng Mananaliksik at Kalahok, I interpreted the growing ease of participation as movement toward a more familiar and trusting relationship.

What the reflection concluded

Service was presented as meaningful, relational, and tied to faith and responsibility.

The reflection concludes that the short placement was meaningful and that the duties of a volunteer teacher, from secular lessons to sharing religious teaching, were carried out with a sense of service and faith.

It expresses a desire to return, know the children more deeply, hear more of their stories, and continue strengthening the relationship formed during the placement.

The paper ends by addressing future Psych 108 students and argues that changing a broken and corrupt system can begin with children who are looking for good teachers, guidance, and opportunities to improve their circumstances.

Reflection

Service becomes formative when reflection does not stop at good intentions.

The original paper captured how I understood the experience at the time. Returning to it now makes another kind of learning possible: preserving what was sincere while also examining the assumptions, language, and position from which I was writing.
