Resistance

Care begins the response. Commitment carries it.

Community work often begins with empathy: seeing another person’s need and deciding that it matters. But empathy alone does not move resources, organize people, bridge distance, or make fragmented services easier to reach.

That requires commitment and coordination. Sometimes that means a small group trusting one another enough to carry an effort across hundreds of kilometers. Sometimes it means bringing institutions with different mandates into the same response.

Through Community, this becomes Resistance: the refusal to let distance, limited resources, institutional boundaries, or imperfect circumstances define the limits of what people can do together.

From service to resistance

Limitations become part of the work, not the end of it.

Resistance in community work does not always look like confrontation. Sometimes it is the distance between where help is gathered and where it is needed. Sometimes it is a shortage of people, time, money, transport, information, or access. Sometimes the barrier is institutional: services exist, but they sit inside separate professions, agencies, and systems.

Those limits reveal what a community is willing to do despite them. They show whether empathy can become commitment, whether trust can hold, and whether people can organize across both personal and institutional boundaries strongly enough to find a way forward.
