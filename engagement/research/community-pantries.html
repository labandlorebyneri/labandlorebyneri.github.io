Research Manuscript · Qualitative Research · June 2021

Pakikipagkapwa and other Filipino values and Individual Filipino’s Perception of Own Responsibility in the Community Pantry Phenomenon Amidst the COVID-19 Crisis

A qualitative study of how organizers and donors understood community pantries through pakikipagkapwa, mutual responsibility, volunteerism, and collective action during the COVID-19 crisis.

The questions

What motivated participation, and how did pakikipagkapwa shape a sense of responsibility?

The study asked what motivated organizers and donors to participate in community pantries, what role pakikipagkapwa played in their perception of personal responsibility, and how they described their experiences within the phenomenon.

Rather than treating community pantries only as emergency food distribution, the paper examined them as a socio-cultural phenomenon shaped by relationships, shared identity, volunteerism, and ideas about responsibility to others.

Method

The study used a virtual focus group to examine meaning as something constructed with others.

The researchers used a qualitative paradigm and a social constructionist approach, augmented by Virgilio Enriquez’s Kapwa Theory. Participants were Filipino community pantry organizers and donors aged 18–59.

Recruitment used purposive and snowball sampling through community-pantry social-media spaces. Ten sign-ups were screened; five participants remained for the focus group after exclusions based on eligibility, willingness, and availability.

The FGD was conducted via Zoom. I served as moderator, Maria Theresa B. Largo handled technical checks and participant support, and Adriel Jireh M. Lumague recorded field notes and observations.

Data analysis used Braun and Clarke’s six-step thematic analysis. Researchers first familiarized themselves with the transcript and field notes, generated initial codes individually, and then met to retain, combine, or revise themes.

What the study found

Four themes organized the analysis.

The manuscript interprets the community pantry phenomenon through Filipino Psychology, especially kapwa, shared responsibility, volunteerism, mutual support, and collective action.

01

Kapwa as collective action

The analysis describes participants as identifying recipients and fellow volunteers as kapwa rather than detached others. The paper connects this shared identity with responsibility and pagkakaisa.

02

Kapwa as mutual benefit

Community pantries are presented as sources of material and social support. The manuscript describes mutual aid as benefiting recipients, volunteers, and the wider community.

03

Kusa in volunteerism

The paper identifies voluntary action as part of the phenomenon: ordinary citizens chose to organize, donate, and continue helping without waiting for a formal obligation to do so.

04

Collective action as social protest

The manuscript also interprets community pantries as a response to unmet needs and as a collective call for justice and social change during the COVID-19 crisis.

Conclusion

Pakikipagkapwa was treated as both relationship and action.

The manuscript concludes that the community pantry phenomenon cannot be understood only as food distribution. Within the paper's framework, participation was tied to shared identity, responsibility for kapwa, mutual support, voluntary service, and collective action.

Community pantries are therefore presented as a form of Filipino social action in which helping another person is connected to belonging to the same community and responding together to a shared crisis.

Study scope

The findings came from one small qualitative focus group.

The manuscript reports five participants who were community pantry organizers or donors. Data came from one virtual focus group conducted during pandemic restrictions.

The study used thematic analysis, researcher reflexivity, field notes, collaborative theme review, and participant debriefing or member checking. Its claims were developed from the experiences represented in that qualitative sample.

From manuscript to public conversation

The study later entered a national Filipino Psychology conference.

The research was later presented at the 45th National Conference for Filipino Psychology in November 2021.

The conference presentation used a different public-facing title, but it came from the same body of work. That distinction is useful on this site: this page documents the manuscript itself, while the related case documents what happened when the research entered scholarly conversation.
