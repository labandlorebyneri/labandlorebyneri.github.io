Suportahan Taka: Understanding The Motivations, Experiences, And The Role Of Filipino Values In The Participation Of Filipino Presidential Candidate Group Volunteers For The Upcoming 2022 Philippine Presidential Elections

Manuscript date

Lead author

Qualitative research · social constructionist perspective

Analysis

The questions

What motivated volunteers, what did they experience, and what role did Filipino values play?

The study asked why Filipinos joined presidential candidate volunteer groups, how they experienced participation in those groups, and how Filipino values shaped their involvement during the period leading to the 2022 presidential election.

Its objectives were to surface volunteer motivations, describe lived experiences, and identify the role of Filipino values in campaign participation.

Method

The study used a qualitative survey to examine volunteerism in its social and political setting.

The researchers used a qualitative paradigm and a social constructionist perspective. Participants were recruited from presidential candidate support groups through purposive and snowball sampling.

Ten respondents completed a Filipino-language survey covering volunteerism, motivations, lived experiences, candidate choice, and Filipino values. Responses were analyzed through an inductive thematic process based on Braun and Clarke's six-step framework.

The paper also drew on Patron-Client Systems Theory and Functional Motivation Theory to interpret relationships between volunteers, candidates, personal motivations, and collective goals.

What the study found

Five themes organized the findings.

These themes summarize the manuscript's analysis of the motivations, experiences, and Filipino values reported by the ten participants.

01

Political Participation that Extends Beyond the Self

Participants described volunteerism through unity, community, fulfillment, convictions, and patriotism. The paper interprets participation as something directed toward goals larger than the individual.

02

Perceived Individual Responsibility

Volunteers described initiative, sacrifice, and non-reciprocity as part of supporting a candidate. They also saw correcting misinformation and introducing the candidate to others as personal responsibilities.

03

Individual Role in Enacting Change

The study reports that participants connected volunteer work with responding to others' needs, taking a principled stance, increasing political awareness, and participating in protest or calls for change.

04

Conditions for Active Involvement

Similarity with fellow volunteers, new friendships, solidarity, belonging, and emotional support were described as conditions that sustained participation.

05

Barriers to Active Involvement

Emotional and physical burnout, inconsistent group interaction, scheduling, and other volunteer roles and responsibilities were identified as barriers.

Interpretation in the manuscript

Bayanihan, responsibility, collective benefit, and political participation were closely connected.

The discussion interprets campaign volunteerism as both personally motivated and socially directed. Participants described their own initiative while also emphasizing community, collective benefit, and responsibility toward others.

The paper connects these patterns to bayanihan and other Filipino values, and uses its theoretical frameworks to describe the relationship between volunteers, electoral candidates, support, loyalty, and expected collective outcomes.

The manuscript also presents the pandemic and increased reliance on digital communication as important conditions shaping how volunteer groups organized and sustained participation.

Conclusion

The paper concludes that campaign volunteerism combined sacrifice, digital organization, bayanihan, and communal political action.

The manuscript draws five conclusions from the study: volunteers made additional sacrifices under pandemic conditions; campaign organizing shifted further toward digital systems; participants perceived the 2022 election as carrying heightened consequences; bayanihan remained important to volunteer participation; and volunteerism was interpreted as a form of communal protest as well as support for a candidate.

The researchers recommend expanding future samples across more presidential campaign groups, using interviews for richer narrative data, and exploring additional bayanihan and Filipino frameworks for analysis.

Study scope

The findings came from ten volunteers and two presidential-campaign volunteer groups.

The manuscript identifies time constraints and the COVID-19 pandemic as important limits on recruitment and data collection. It therefore used a qualitative survey rather than a broader or interview-based design.

The findings represent the experiences of the participating volunteers and the groups from which they were recruited during the study period.
