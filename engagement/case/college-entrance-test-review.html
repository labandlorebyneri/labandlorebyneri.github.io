College Entrance Test Review

A story about what separates people when intelligence is not the issue: access, preparation, confidence, and the opportunity to imagine that a place like UP could also be meant for someone from a small farming town.

Year: 2019

← Back to Education & Development

Why this case matters

Opportunity is not the same thing as intelligence.

I grew up in a simple town where farming is one of the main sources of livelihood. For a long time, institutions like the University of the Philippines felt distant, almost like they belonged to another kind of student from another kind of place.

I never thought I would qualify for UP. When I did, something shifted. It was not evidence that I had suddenly become more capable. It was evidence that geography had never been a measure of intelligence in the first place.

What often separates students is opportunity: access to information, preparation, mentors, resources, and the chance to see themselves in places they have been taught to think are far away.

Documentary record

The opportunity became something I could pass forward.

The return

The review program was not repayment out of obligation.

When I returned to St. John's Academy and worked with the Office of Guidance and Counselling on a college entrance test review, I did not see it as something I owed anyone. I saw it as gratitude made practical.

I gathered people who could teach Mathematics Proficiency, Science Proficiency, Reading Comprehension, Language Ability, and Test-Taking Strategies. I handled Test-Taking Strategies myself, helped prepare the materials, and also processed students' admission applications.

The review sessions themselves ran across four Saturdays. The broader work extended beyond those sessions through preparation beforehand and post-review support afterward, including preparing materials, helping with admission requirements, and assisting students with the submission of documents.

Across those four Saturdays, the work was ordinary in the best sense: lessons, practice, explanations, and repeated reminders that difficult things become more manageable when someone helps make the path visible.

What changed for me

Qualifying for UP widened the map.

The most important thing UP gave me at that moment was not a claim to be more intelligent than anyone else. It gave me proof that the boundary I had imagined between a student from the countryside and a national university was not fixed.

Once I saw that possibility for myself, it became difficult not to see it for other people from where I came from. I wanted students in a town shaped by farms, public markets, families making do, and ordinary work to know that ambition did not have to be imported from somewhere else.

Someone from a small town could belong in those rooms too.

Reflection

The separation line was opportunity.

I do not believe students from the countryside are less intelligent than students from urban places. What differs far too often is what they are exposed to, what preparation is available, who tells them a path is possible, and whether someone helps them cross the first few unfamiliar steps.

The College Entrance Test Review was my way of returning something I had received: not a guarantee of success, but a wider sense of what could be attempted.

What I carry forward

Gratitude can become infrastructure.

Gratitude does not always have to be expressed as a thank-you. Sometimes it can become a class, a Saturday morning, an application form, a test strategy, a set of materials, or one more person willing to say, "Try. You may belong there too."

That is what I remember most about this engagement. The goal was never to prove that everyone would reach the same destination. It was to make sure geography did not decide who was allowed to imagine the journey.
