Creativity × Behavioral Science

Creativity belongs in science.

It is often assumed that creativity and science sit at opposite poles. As a creative behavioral scientist, I reject that distinction. I see creativity as an input to breakthrough science.

Great painters relied on geometry, proportion, perspective, and measurement to create enduring masterpieces. At the same time, artificial intelligence, one of today's most consequential creative technologies, emerges from rules, logic, data, and computation.

The relationship between creativity and scientific thinking is not a contradiction. It is often where discovery begins.

I study how people think, decide, create, and interact with technology, as individuals, in groups, across society, and through movements.

The Three Cs

Three lenses for understanding human behavior.

Lab & Lore organizes my observations, questions, experiments, and reflections around three interconnected dimensions of human life: Character, Creativity, and Community.

01 · The Individual

Character

Who we become.

Reflections on the human psyche, identity, interests, attitudes, values, morality, decision-making, pain, suffering, personal growth, leadership, and the forces that shape the individual.

02 · The Response

Creativity

How we respond.

Ideas about marketing, communication, experimentation, research, problem solving, organizational behavior, and alternative ways of approaching difficult questions and uncertain situations.

03 · The Collective

Community

How we move together.

Reflections on group thinking, group dynamics, leadership, organizational behavior, hierarchy, social and economic mobility, collective action, communities, society, and the formation of movements.

The Idea Behind Lab & Lore

Character. Creativity. Community.

These are the three lenses through which I try to understand the world.

The lab is where I observe, question, test, examine, challenge, and occasionally change my mind.

The lore is what remains after experience, reflection, evidence, and time.

What is understood in the lab becomes lore for another generation to encounter, question, and build upon.
